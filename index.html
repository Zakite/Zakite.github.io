I am a PhD candidate in Earth and Planetary Sciences at Rutgers University, working with Dr. Shaunna M. Morrison.

I am passionate about understanding how biology influences mineral formation across scales, from molecular self-assembly and crystal-lattice development to the mesostructures that emerge as crystallites assemble.

From solution to organized.

Before a crystal exists

Using guanine as a model system, I investigate how cation identity and solution chemistry govern molecular self-assembly, beginning with ion-mediated G-wire formation and bundling. I then follow the development of these precursors into crystals through crystallization by particle attachment (CPA), followed by Ostwald ripening during crystal maturation. Parallel uric acid experiments serve as a comparative control, helping distinguish general ion-mediated assembly from guanine's Hoogsteen-mediated self-affinity. By isolating these processes in completely abiotic systems, I aim to identify fundamental mechanisms of particle assembly and crystal maturation with potential relevance across diverse biomineral systems.

Crystallization by particle attachment.

Crystals can grow not only through the addition of individual ions and molecules, but also through the attachment of larger, preassembled species. These pathways span molecular clusters, dense liquid precursors, amorphous particles, and nanocrystals. Their assembly and attachment can influence the structure and organization of the resulting solid.

My work on guanine and urate investigates the early stages of this process, where ion-regulated molecular assemblies and partially ordered precursors may shape subsequent crystallization.
