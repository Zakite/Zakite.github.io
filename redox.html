Redox ladder

The scale runs from H2 at the bottom to O2 at the top. Energy for life is the gap between two rungs, in kJ per mole of electrons. Rungs are placed in volts on the hydrogen scale (SHE).

Redox rank against O2

For ranking minerals from reduced to oxidized. kJ per mole of electrons, O2 = 0. Positive: O2 can oxidize it. Negative: it can only be reduced. Zero: inert.

How it works

  • Input. The mineral’s Gibbs energy of formation, measured, from twelve published databases. Every IMA mineral can be looked up by name. If it has no measured value, the energy is estimated from its IMA formula and flagged. The ± shows how far the value could be off.
  • Reaction. At the redox potential the partner sets, energy minimization finds the most stable products in water at your pH.
  • Answer. The gap between the mineral and the partner on the redox ladder, in volts and in kJ per mole of electrons. YES is colored by the energy per mole of partner reaction (at least two electrons): green at 20 kJ or more (the cost of moving one ion across a membrane, Schink 1997), yellow from 10 to 20 (the lowest microbes are seen living on, Hoehler et al. 2001), red under 10.
  • Electrons only. Energy a mineral releases just by sitting in water (dissolving, hydrating, turning into a more stable form), with every element keeping its oxidation state, is not redox energy. It is reported on its own and left out of every value here.
  • Rank. The same calculation against O2 only. Size: how far the mineral’s redox boundary sits below O2, in kJ per mole of electrons (96.485 × the gap in volts). Sign: positive if the mineral is the reduced side of that boundary (O2 oxidizes it), negative if it is the oxidized side. A metal and its oxide share one boundary and get opposite signs.
  • Limit. Thermodynamics only. It does not say an organism has the enzymes, or that the reaction is fast.
